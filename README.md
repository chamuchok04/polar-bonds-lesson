# Polar and Non-polar Covalent Bonds: an interactive lesson

An interactive study page for **ICSE Class 8** students (introductory level) on
polar and non-polar covalent bonds. Everything is in one file: `index.html`.

## What's inside

1. **Learn**: 10 short slides with animated diagrams. The first slide opens
   with a 3D-style oxygen atom: a nucleus of 8 protons, with electrons spinning
   in see-through shells, drawn live on a canvas. The slides cover an atoms
   and bonding recap, covalent bonds, electronegativity, non-polar and polar
   bonds, the ΔEN rule of thumb, worked examples, water and oil, a summary,
   and an optional CO₂ challenge slide.
2. **Explore**
   - **Bond Builder**: pick any two atoms and see the shared electron pair move,
     the δ+ / δ− labels, a ΔEN meter and the calculation written out.
   - **Tug-of-War**: sliders for each atom's electronegativity move the
     "electron-pair flag" live.
   - **Molecule Gallery**: H₂, O₂, Cl₂, HCl, H₂O, NH₃, CH₄ and CO₂ (challenge).
   - **Electronegativity Map**: 18 elements placed by their real group and
     period, coloured from blue (weak pull) through cream to rust (strong
     pull). Tapping an element shows its atom (nucleus, shells and electrons),
     its shell arrangement, its electronegativity, and a short "tug-of-war"
     explanation of why its pull is weak, moderate or strong. On a wide screen
     the details sit beside the map; on a phone they appear below it.
3. **Quiz**: 10 multiple-choice questions with hints, explanations, a score
   and a list of answers to review. "Try again" shuffles the questions and
   the answer options.

A small **Notes** button in the top-right corner opens **Teacher Notes**, a
reference for the teacher only. It covers where the topic sits in the syllabus,
electronegativity trends, common misconceptions, extra analogies, the limits of
the rule of thumb, and ways to answer questions that go beyond the syllabus.

## How to open it

- **On a computer:** download `index.html` and double-click it. It opens in
  any modern browser (Chrome, Edge, Firefox, Safari).
- **On a phone:** open the file from your downloads or a shared drive.
- It works fully **offline**. It needs no internet, no login and no
  installation, and it saves or sends nothing.
- All animations are drawn by the page itself (CSS, SVG and canvas). If the
  device is set to reduce motion, they stay still.
- To move between slides, use the ← / → arrow keys or the Next and Previous
  buttons. The sun/moon button switches between light and dark mode.

## Colour key

- **Blue = δ− (partly negative)**, **Orange = δ+ (partly positive)**. Every
  diagram also shows the text labels, so the page never relies on colour
  alone.
- ● and × stand for the two electrons in a shared pair.
- Two pictures use colour differently, and each says so on screen:
  - On the opening atom, orange → blue shows **shell depth** (inner → outer),
    not charge.
  - On the Electronegativity Map, blue → rust shows **pulling strength**, not
    charge.

## Science notes

- Electronegativity values are on the Pauling scale: H 2.20, C 2.55, N 3.04,
  O 3.44, F 3.98, Cl 3.16, Br 2.96, I 2.66, S 2.58, P 2.19, Na 0.93, K 0.82,
  Mg 1.31, Li 0.98. The Electronegativity Map also uses Be 1.57, B 2.04,
  Al 1.61 and Si 1.90.
- The ΔEN cut-offs (below 0.4 non-polar, 0.4 to 1.7 polar, above 1.7 mostly
  ionic) are presented as a **rule of thumb**. The Bond Builder points out the
  known exceptions: H–F and P–F are covalent even though ΔEN is just above 1.7,
  metal + non-metal pairs are usually ionic, and pairs of metals form metallic
  bonds.
